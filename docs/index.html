Building training scenes from what the robot actually did

A Unitree G1 walking a corridor meets an overhead beam. It can keep walking, or commit to a crouch. Teaching it to choose needs encounters where the choice has consequences — so we construct those encounters from the robot's own executed motion, label them with paired physics, and then measure which part of that construction actually teaches the decision.

The idea, in one execution pair

Same scene, same seed, two commands — forced, not selected by a learner

That pair is the unit of supervision. A scene is worth labelling when the robot's own executed alternatives disagree there — one clears, another strikes. The research question is whether constructing scenes that way teaches a sensor-driven policy to choose better than simpler constructions do.

The proposed method

Four stages. Each is falsifiable by the next, and stage four is where the claim lives.

  1. Execute the alternatives, don't imagine themIn an empty room the frozen SONIC tracker runs every command in a qualified repertoire — neutral walking plus six timed crouch schedules. We keep the achieved 50 Hz whole-body trajectories, not the references the controller was asked to track.
  2. Place the obstacle where those executions disagreeA candidate scene is one where some schedule keeps clearance across all 81 placement offsets (the positive screen) and some other schedule nominally interferes (the negative screen). Geometry proposes; it never labels.
  3. Let physics assign the labelWith the beam present, all seven schedules are executed. An encounter is useful only if the outcome table contains a verified continuation the teacher can recommend — passage under a 1 N contact criterion with 0.3 s of upright stability.
  4. Train one fixed learner and evaluate it by executionA per-phase ridge readout over a 114-dimensional causal sensor/state vector picks a complete schedule at one of three decision ticks. Privileged scene parameters and future outcomes are teaching signal only, never policy inputs. Then we run it in physics.

Every protocol is registered with its predictions filed before the physics runs, failed predictions are retained rather than removed, and every artifact is hash-bound to the sources that produced it. Where a prediction failed, this page says so.

The only arm executed contrast beats is target-only, the weakest. Any "no reverse case" generalisation fails at this layer.

The strong scripted comparator passes 6 of 6 and sits at this panel's ceiling, so a tie in success count is a property of the panel, not evidence that the populations are equal. What the panel does establish is that the distinctive ingredient did not change behaviour: thirteen of fifteen learned policies collapsed to a constant schedule, and none passed anywhere the seven-schedule bank failed.

Reconciling the two generations gives the finding that actually survives. Executed-contrast construction beats unscreened placement decisively, and does not beat feasibility-screened placement at all. The part carrying the measured benefit is the positive executable-support screen. The additional negative-interference targeting — the distinctive Motion2Scene ingredient — has no measured downstream effect.

Why the corpora collapsed

The two corpora that no single fixed schedule can cover are exactly the two whose policies used more than one schedule; all thirteen single-schedule-coverable corpora trained constant policies. The correspondence holds on 15 of 15 corpora. A Fisher exact figure of p ≈ 0.0095 is recorded in the decision note as prose; it appears in no measured artifact, and two qualifications belong with it: the fifteen corpora are 5 arms × 3 seeds over six shared contexts and one execution seed, so they are not fifteen independent draws, and the hypothesis was formed after seeing the table. Corpus complementarity is also necessary but not sufficient — one non-constant policy used three schedules and still selected the wrong one on the only context that discriminates. Non-constancy is a diagnostic, never the objective.

Two method components remain untested rather than supported. Complete-continuation teaching was common to all five arms, so nothing in this panel isolates it. Historical observation-gated replay is a measured null — identical to executed contrast in all eighteen cells, down to the selected schedule — and is reported as a tested component, not a contribution.

Relaxing the threshold all the way to zero interference buys one additional encounter. The binding constraint is recall, not threshold: two of the encounters the screen misses have covering schedules predicted clear that physically failed, so no negative margin can ever reach them, and a third is rejected by the positive robustness bar instead. The threshold hypothesis is therefore closed rather than promising.

The 2-of-2 precision figure is retrospective and selection-conditioned on encounters that were chosen for labelling by this very screen. It is not an established population precision, and the threshold sweep is not held-out calibration.

What is running right now

One bounded intervention, registered before any of its physics — and with no result yet

If the positive screen carries the benefit and the negative screen has poor recall, the natural test is to keep the executed geometry as a proposal bias while allowing bounded exploration outside the gate. Three nested acquisition controls draw from one fresh reservoir of 3,840 candidates in the same declared task domain:

All three arms import a byte-identical four-encounter prefix and the same M4 checkpoint, so exactly one thing varies: which channel proposes each of four new encounters. The tracker, the seven schedules, the causal input, the complete-continuation teacher, the ridge learner at λ = 10 and the physics seed are common. 60.4% of the declared domain lies outside the positive robustness bar, so the exploration channel genuinely has support the strict gate cannot reach.

Status as of 10 September 2026: acquisition in flight. 288 new encounter episodes are assigned and being executed; the 200-episode evaluation panel has not run. There is no downstream result, and nothing on this page should be read as one. The evaluation uses a separate ten-context validation sample drawn from task geometry alone — zero clearance queries, zero outcome queries — because one of the six original development contexts was minted by the same predicate a support-driven selector would use, and so cannot confirm this intervention.

The measured operating range

Where the inherited capability holds, and where it breaks

Across 42 registered executions, reactive and scripted privileged crouching each pass 9 of 10 pose trials while all ten blind trials contact the beam. Blind walking still crosses under contact, so contact is not evidence of impassability — the criterion is contact-qualified passage, not mere arrival. The causal observation delay locates the timing budget directly: 100 ms and 260 ms both pass 2/2, while 500 ms refuses and fails 2/2. Both reactive and oracle failures occur at the same condition — station −15 cm in the second seed, with a 429 N contact — so the limit is the geometry of that approach, not the sensing policy alone.

Paper status

Where the manuscripts actually stand, and which of two papers the evidence supports

No current draft contains the five-arm result. Three drafts exist: a 20-page working manuscript (9 Sep), the only 8-page conference-format draft (8 Sep), and a superseded 7 Sep draft that carries its own banner because three of its claim families assert exactly the downstream benefit the five-arm panel measured as zero. All three predate the panel. Reconciling the manuscript with the null is the most urgent writing task, ahead of any new experiment.

The recorded venue is ICRA 2027, eight pages including references, closing 15 September 2026. Of the five items below, only the first fits that window at ≈7.6 GPU-hours. Items 2 through 5 do not, and this page does not pretend otherwise.

Methods paper — the contribution has narrowed and is still real

Target: ICRA 2027. The abstract and performance claims are limited to completed development evidence.

The defensible claim today is mechanistic rather than performance-based: executed-motion construction reliably concentrates physical labelling on solvable adaptation-required encounters, and that is not sufficient to teach perceptive selection. What distinguishes the corpora that trained selective policies is response complementarity across the corpus, and the executed-envelope screen predicts that property with high precision and low recall. The acquisition-stage yield advantage is not the downstream result, and this page does not present it as one.

have: five-arm panel, screen calibration, mechanism account

System / dataset paper — assets exist, reuse evidence does not

Branch-linked teacher and student data with hash-bound provenance is a genuine companion asset.

1,065 recorded captures across fifteen corpora, hash-bound registrations for every stage, and one portable release that is exported and offline-verifiable: 12 corpora, 276 episodes, 328,992 measured physics steps, reconstructed to a maximum coefficient difference of 5.55e-17. No M8, M16 or M32 release has been exported, and the archive-integrity counts are verification totals over that 276-episode slice, not dataset scale. What is missing is the part that makes a dataset paper: a demonstration that the corpus is useful to a different learner or robot, plus licensing and a public reproduction harness. The research compass is explicit that new-learner or new-robot utility requires additional evidence, and none has been collected.

have: corpus, exporter, provenance; missing: reuse demo, licence

What remains

In dependency order. Each item states what it would settle, not merely what it would produce.

1 · Finish the support-preserving pilot

Because it is the smallest test of the one mechanism the calibration actually implicated.

Complete the 288 acquisition episodes and the 200-episode validation panel. It settles whether restoring proposal support outside the negative screen changes physically executed passage, and it distinguishes "broadening support helps" from "the guided half adds value". If action diversity improves without passage improving, the central hypothesis stays unsupported.

in flight · ~7.6 GPU-hours total

2 · Protected held-out evaluation

Because every result so far is development or regression evidence.

Eighteen reserved layouts are geometry-locked with their generation recorded at zero clearance queries and zero physics steps. They have never been executed, and eight of nine adoption gates remain unmet — the implementation lock and the finite-horizon terminal-censoring policy must be fixed before a single episode runs. This is the difference between a development claim and a held-out one.

have: locked geometry + hashes; blocked: adoption gates

3 · Source-held-out transfer

Because every evaluation so far varies layout, not motion ancestry.

Reserved source identifiers are registered against a provenance ledger and none has been generated or qualified. Acquiring them and re-running the comparison with ancestry held out is the single largest gap between the present claim and a general one, and it needs no new method.

have: reserved IDs, qualification protocol

4 · Replace ideal sensing with the deployed pipeline

Because the learner currently reads exact ray returns from a scripted sensor.

Re-acquire the same encounters through the real depth or lidar path with its noise, dropout and field of view, and refit the identical learner. The measured 500 ms delay failure already bounds the timing budget; sensor visibility of the critical moment is the quantity expected to degrade first.

have: delay panel, per-construction visibility

5 · Sequential decisions, then hardware qualification

Because the policy commits once from a fixed approach, and a real robot decides repeatedly.

Closing the loop changes the state distribution the learner sees, so it requires matched data aggregation across every arm and fresh labels. Only then does hardware become meaningful — and nothing here is a protective action: a refusal continues walking. The 1 N contact criterion is a simulation convention, not a safety margin. The C++ TensorRT runtime exists, but no Motion2Scene hardware transfer evidence exists at all.

have: deploy stack, timing window, legal-transition manager

What this is not

Everything here is simulation: one overhead-beam family along a fixed approach, seven qualified schedules, three decision phases, at most one adaptation followed by recovery, ideal ray sensing, and one frozen inherited controller whose locomotion capability is not a contribution of this work.

Every completed evaluation on this page is development or regression evidence. No result reported here is protected held-out: layouts were withheld from fitting, but some had published outcomes before the learner form was chosen, and source ancestry was never varied. A protected held-out evaluation is item 2 on the remaining-work list, not a property of any result shown. Acceptance was judged at the exact pose the simulator authors, so nothing here is uncertainty-robust. No claim is made about general navigation, steering, protective stopping, repeated skill chaining, hardware transfer, or energy efficiency. Passage time is crossing plus stabilization, not full maneuver completion.

A hand-written ray rule remains a legitimate and strong comparator, and on the larger panel it beats every learned arm on success count. We report that direction too.

Reproducing it

The five-arm acquisition recorded 1,264,988 measured physics steps across 1,065 captures, taken from its own boundary receipts. Its evaluation panel scored 138 of 138 assignments — 105 passes, 33 failures, no unrun and no technically-missing cells. We deliberately do not quote a step total for the panel: 164,496 is its declared assignment ceiling, and the study protocol forbids summing that ceiling as though it were a measurement. Construction, screening and fitting are CPU-only and take minutes — the pilot's entire 3,840-candidate reservoir cost 999,424 clearance queries and zero physics. The cost is Isaac Lab execution, at a measured 56.5 s median per 1,192-step episode on a single RTX 5080 (p95 60.3 s). Earlier notes quoting ~32 s describe a retired shorter horizon and do not apply to this path.

Raw run records, admission audits, fitted models and hash manifests are published beside each result, and every registered protocol states its predictions before the physics runs.
