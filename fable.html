Update: after executing this plan

Written 09:00 EDT, September 7. The plan below was written at 00:30 and has now largely been executed by an autonomous supervisor plus this session. Five things are settled; two change the paper. Everything further down still stands except where this section overrides it.

A · The panel ran

The transition bank finished 12/12 at 0.105 GPU-hours with 41001, 41002 and 41003 qualified at both seeds. The four-arm study was registered, 82 of 82 labels acquired, four learners fitted, and 366 of 540 evaluations admitted before other projects filled the shared card.

B · The outcome is none of A, B or C. Call it D.

Under the registered contract the learned arm acquired zero generated groups and the analytic arm one. Three of the four fitted learners are behaviourally identical: uniform, target-only and Motion2Scene issue walk on all 61 conditions and pass 21/61. The analytic learner issues the crouch 22 times and passes 30/61, with 9 wins and 0 losses against each of the others, consistent on all three carriers. Removing its single useful label drops its adaptation requests from 22 to zero and makes it bit-identical to the background-only model. The entire measured effect rests on one physically verified contrast, so the paper's real subject is contrast scarcity.

Thirty-six groups are assigned and 72 label commands are prepared, waiting on the card.

D · A second defect does belong to the generator

On 41003 the inherited envelope still leaves 32 witnesses and the learned draws reached none of them. Across the nominal proposals only 24 of 34 of the learned arm's critical scenes are visible to the sensor at the 0.30 s decision, against 44 of 47 for analytic, with 8 of 12 invisible on 41003 alone. Placing a contrast where the robot cannot see it is a real quality gap, independent of the envelope, and gated by neither contract. Report it; do not add a visibility gate after the fact.

E · Adaptation is nearly free here, and every learner under-uses it

Of the 38 conditions where both commands were executed there are 15 crouch-only successes, 11 both-pass, 12 both-fail and zero walk-only successes. The scripted ray rule requests the crouch 38 times and reaches 36/61, exactly the best-of-observed ceiling; the analytic learner requests it 22 times and reaches 30/61. A hand-written rule still beats every learned arm. Say so plainly, and note that the pending background suites are where unnecessary adaptation gets charged, so the ceiling is not the whole story.

Revised thesis. Not "learned beats analytic", and not "construction matters" in the abstract. It is: a training scene is useful only if it contains a decision-relevant contrast that survives execution and is visible at decision time; such scenes are geometrically scarce, the scarcity is measurable and designable, and one verified contrast is enough to change a fixed learner's behaviour.
Revised order of remaining work. Let the supervisor finish the 174 outstanding runs; it is blocked only by other projects holding the card and self-resumes. Then run the 72 prepared nominal label commands (~0.6 GPU-h), fit, and evaluate at one seed (144 runs, ~1.3 GPU-h), reusing the existing comparators. Decide on September 10 from the combined funnel. Remaining total is about 3.4 GPU-hours. The binding risk is no longer scope or budget: three other projects hold 13.5 of the card's 16.3 GB. If that persists through September 8, drop the nominal evaluation and publish the nominal acquisition funnel alone, which is already a complete result.

Verdict, as written at 00:30

  1. The project has a working embodied pipeline, exact reproducibility, and an unusually honest failure ledger. It does not have a paper, and its intended headline (a learned generator supplies better training scenes than analytic construction) is currently contradicted by its own data: all nine Motion2Scene training scenes are both-fail, and the only measured learned rescue came from the analytic arm.
  2. The learner tie was a bug, not a finding. The 0.001 normalization clamp on 70 constant dimensions inflated test inputs to 1952.75; the linear control on physical scales fixed it. The M2S data failure has a diagnosed, cheap cause: proposals were screened against the reference trajectory, and the achieved-transition screen catches all 13 false-clears. Both fixes exist. Neither has yet been run as data.
  3. Compute is not the constraint. A cell costs about 33 s (0.0094 GPU-h); the whole project has spent 4.3 GPU-h; the whole ICRA experiment below costs about 6 to 8 GPU-h. The constraint is scope discipline and GPU contention: the 7500 MiB floor already stalled one batch at 0/20 while another job held 4.2 GB.
  4. Change the thesis now. Not "learned beats analytic". Instead: training scenes constructed from executed motion contrasts, with paired physical labels, teach a perceptive selector when to adapt, and here is which part of the construction does the work. Learned vs analytic proposal becomes one factor in a four-arm ablation, and every outcome of that factor is reportable.
  5. Cut to one budget, four arms, one learner, at most three carriers, the twelve frozen layouts. Drop 24/48/96, the fifth arm, optimizer seeds (the learner is deterministic), and the 9490xxx final-source acquisition. Those are the RA-L or IROS extension.
  6. Decision point is Sep 10, 18:00. If the transition-aware M2S arm yields at least one useful contrast per carrier on two or more carriers, write the four-arm paper. If a second carrier never qualifies or M2S is again all both-fail, still write the narrowed paper, but expect modest acceptance odds and decide in writing whether the same manuscript goes to RA-L.

The paper you can honestly write by Sep 15

Title and thesis

Motion2Scene: Constructing Training Scenes from Executed Humanoid Motion Contrasts. Keep "Transition-Aware Counterfactual Environments" as a subtitle only if the transition-aware arm wins.

A humanoid's executed motion contrast, the same approach with and without an adaptation, both run through the frozen controller, identifies where an obstacle must sit to make the adaptation useful. We construct training scenes at those locations, label each with the paired physical outcome of both commands, and show that a fixed perceptive selector trained on such scenes learns when to adapt, while selectors trained on uniform or contrast-free scenes do not. We ablate which parts of the construction matter: execution-verified versus reference-based conditioning, contrast versus target-only objectives, and learned versus analytic proposal.

This is true today for the analytic arm on one carrier. The experiment below tests it on two to three carriers with all four arms. Its strength is that every outcome of the learned-versus-analytic factor is a finding, so the paper cannot be killed by that result.

Reviewer objections and the honest answers

  • "One beam and two commands." Yes; say so in the first paragraph of Limitations and in the abstract as a controlled minimal instance. The value is the protocol and the ablation, not breadth.
  • "Ideal rays, no perception." Declared. The scripted-ray comparator bounds what perception could add; the 500 ms result shows the selector is timing-limited.
  • "n = 2–3 sources." Paired design, all cells shown, carrier-grouped reporting, no pooled percentages. Source-held-out transfer is the named next study.
  • "The learned generator doesn't beat the analytic one." If outcome B, that is the finding, and more useful than a marginal win. State what the learned model does buy, with numbers.
  • "Why not RL, a diffusion baseline, hardware?" Out of scope by design; one sentence each.

Rules this project has already paid for

  • Register before spend; predictions first; infra failure is not a rejection; no uncharged retries.
  • Physics is the verdict; screens, forecasts and MuJoCo replays never re-grade.
  • One decision time (0.30 s); old 0.20 s labels are not 0.30 s labels.
  • Fixed learner across arms; any learner change applies to every arm and is declared.
  • Derivatives, physics repeats and layouts stay with their carrier; report grouped.
  • Retained failures go in the paper, not only in the ledger.
  • Never let a test-bank failure tune generation, sensing or the controller.
